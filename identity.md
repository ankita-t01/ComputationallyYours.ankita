## A · The condition

**The condition.** The need to carry information discreetly across political conflicts in seventeenth-century Maharashtra. A message could pass through many hands before reaching its intended reader. Maya’s work asks how a painting or poem might carry information without announcing that it contains a secret.

**Who lives inside it.** People entrusted with sensitive news. Messengers who travel between a fort and the settlements around it. Families who wait for news from someone away. People whose safety depends on knowing when to speak and when to remain silent.

**Three pieces of evidence.**

- Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj made Raigad Fort his capital in 1674. His coronation took place there on 6 June of that year. ([Government of India, Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/FeaturesDeatils.aspx?ModuleId=2&NoteId=153371&lang=2&reg=48))
- A collection of historical letters relating to Shivaji includes letters addressed to him or his father Shahji, letters written by secretaries, and manuscript reports from courts and camps. ([*Historical Letters Relating to Shivaji*, hosted by IGNCA](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/35126.pdf))
- The *Nujum al-Ulum*, an illustrated manuscript produced in Bijapur in 1570, is an early dated example of Deccani manuscript painting. It comes from a different court and an earlier period than Maya’s setting, but offers a starting point for researching the wider region’s visual traditions. ([Salar Jung Museum, *Women in Deccani Painting*](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/women-in-deccani-painting-salar-jung-museum/KwXBj1uyVCU9Ag?hl=en))

Maya and her particular code are fictional. These sources ground her world in a historical place, surviving correspondence and regional artwork; they do not establish that messages were hidden in paintings in the way she uses them.

## B · The displacement

**Coordinates moved:** role and time.

**Where she stands.** A messenger and spy connected to Raigad in seventeenth-century Maharashtra. She travels between a contact inside the fort and a trusted ally outside it, carrying messages hidden in paintings and poetry.

**What can be seen from there.** A message has more than one audience. The person it is meant for may notice a missing flower or an unusual line of verse. Someone else may see only a gift, a picture or a poem. Maya has to think about what each person will notice, including the people she hopes will notice nothing.

## C · The facts

**Name, and why.** Maya. The name gives her a distinct identity. Its association with illusion suits a person whose work can be read in more than one way. I still need to research how I want to use that association in her historical setting.

**Origin.** Maya grew up near Raigad among people who travelled between the fort and nearby settlements. She learned to observe small changes in the landscape and to pay attention to the things people left unsaid. Her connection to the fort eventually brought her work as a messenger.

**Practice.** She receives a short message from a trusted contact and translates it into details within a painting or a verse. She carries the work to its recipient, who knows how to read it. After delivery, the same painting can remain in view as an ordinary object.

**Tools and materials.** Paper, ink, brushes and pigments; handwritten verses; folded letters; and a small collection of agreed signs. The precise materials Maya would have had access to need further historical research.

**Lineage to research.**

- Painting and decorative traditions associated with seventeenth-century Maharashtra.
- Marathi poetry and written correspondence from the period.
- Examples of hidden messages in art or writing, considered carefully for their time and place.

## D · Attention

**What she attends to.** The distance between two birds. A flower missing from a border. A word repeated where it does not need to be. The order in which a person reads an image. Whether the painting looks ordinary enough to be carried.

**What she refuses.** A code so obvious that it invites someone to solve it. Symbols whose meanings are explained on the surface of the work. Details included only because they look historical. A painting that sacrifices its appearance as a believable object for the sake of the secret.

**What she would build.** A collection of paintings that can each be read in two ways: as an image by anyone who sees it, and as a message by the person who knows what to look for.

## E · Beliefs and rules

**Three beliefs.**

- A message is safest when people do not realise there is a message.
- A small detail can carry more than an explanation written plainly.
- Trust depends on what the sender and receiver agree to notice.

**Three rules.**

- Because the message must remain hidden, the work must still read as a painting when the code is unknown.
- Because the receiver needs to understand it, each message must follow a consistent set of agreed rules.
- Because Maya cannot control who sees the work, the painting must never display the secret message as plain text.

## F · The distance

**What is shared.** An interest in artwork, poetry and the way images can communicate without explaining everything. The experience of noticing that a small detail can change how something feels or what it means.

**Where we differ.** Maya lives in a time of political conflict and carries messages whose consequences are part of her daily life. I am designing her from the present and can explore her world through research and imagination. What is a creative experiment for me would be a responsibility and a risk for her.

**What has to be found out.** What kinds of paper, pigments and visual traditions were available around Raigad in her period. How messages were carried. Who travelled between the fort and surrounding settlements, and what forms of poetry or correspondence they used.
